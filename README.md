What Are Criminal Cases?

Under the Indian Penal Code, 1860, a criminal offence occurs when a person voluntarily commits an act prohibited by law with a guilty intention. An act alone is not enough; it must be accompanied by a guilty mind (mens rea).

This principle is expressed in the legal maxim “actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea”, meaning the act does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty. When the police register an offence and the matter proceeds to court, it becomes a criminal case.

The Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC) classifies offences into four main categories:

Cognizable

Non-cognizable

Bailable

Non-bailable

Each category follows a different legal procedure.

Types of Criminal Cases
Bailable Offences

Defined under Section 2(a) of the CrPC, these offences are punishable with imprisonment of less than three years or a fine. The accused has an absolute right to bail, subject to conditions set by the court.

Non-Bailable Offences

These offences are serious in nature, punishable with death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment exceeding seven years. Bail is not a matter of right and is granted at the discretion of the court, which must record reasons for its decision.

Cognizable Offences

These are serious offences where the police can register an FIR and arrest without a warrant. Such offences attract stricter punishments under the IPC.

Non-Cognizable Offences

These are less serious offences where police cannot arrest without a court’s permission. The complainant must approach the appropriate court to initiate proceedings.
